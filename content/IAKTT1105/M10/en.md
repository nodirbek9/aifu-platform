## Why does this topic matter?

Getting a certificate, registering a company or submitting a tax report used to mean queues, paper documents and visits to several offices. **E-government** makes these services online, fast and transparent. For an economist it is an everyday working tool: tax reports, electronic invoices, statistical reports, taking part in public procurement — all through electronic systems.

:::note
**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- explain the concept, goals and models of e-government;

- know the main elements of e-government infrastructure;
- use the main electronic public services in Uzbekistan;
- assess the effectiveness and risks of e-government.
:::

## Concept and models

**E-government** is the interaction of government bodies with citizens, businesses and other agencies through ICT; its goal is to provide services quickly, conveniently and transparently, reduce costs and lower the risk of corruption.

| Model | With whom | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **G2C** (government — citizen) | citizens | certificates, applications, payments, booking appointments |
| **G2B** (government — business) | entrepreneurs | registering a company, tax reports, licences, public procurement |
| **G2G** (government — government) | between agencies | inter-agency data exchange, electronic document workflow |
| **G2E** (government — employee) | civil servants | HR systems, training, internal portals |

## Stages of development

| Stage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1. **Information** | information, laws and contacts on agency websites |
| 2. **Interaction** | downloading application forms, electronic requests |
| 3. **Transaction** | receiving and paying for a service fully online (with a digital signature) |
| 4. **Transformation** | agencies are connected: a citizen does not submit data again and again (the "once-only" principle), proactive services (for example, a benefit paid automatically when a child is born) |

## Infrastructure

- **A single portal** — one entry point to all services.
- **Identification** — the OneID single identification system, digital signature keys, the ID card chip, mobile identification (in detail in M10 of the KIA course).
- **Inter-agency integration** — a platform for secure data exchange; a citizen does not bring a certificate, the agency requests it directly.
- **Electronic payments** — paying state fees and fines online.
- **Open data** — publishing government statistics and data in machine-readable formats (data.gov.uz).
- **Data centres and information security**.

## E-government in Uzbekistan

| System | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **my.gov.uz** — the Single Portal of Interactive Public Services | hundreds of public services online (certificates, permits, registration) |
| **Public service centres** | a "one-stop shop" — electronic services with offline help |
| **The tax service's electronic account** (soliq.uz) | reports, payments, checking tax debt |
| **The electronic invoice system** | exchanging invoices between companies with digital signatures |
| **Public procurement portals** | electronic tenders and auctions |
| **The open data portal** (data.gov.uz) | government datasets |
| **Electronic queues, e-education and e-health systems** | enrolling in school, kindergarten, booking a doctor |

Legal basis: the Law "On E-Government" (2015), the laws "On Electronic Digital Signature" and "On Personal Data", and the "Digital Uzbekistan — 2030" strategy.

**International assessment**: the UN **E-Government Development Index** (EGDI) rates countries on online services, telecommunications infrastructure and human capital; it is published every two years. Leading experience: Estonia (the X-Road data exchange system, digital identity), Denmark, South Korea, Singapore (in detail in MT18).

## Effectiveness and risks

| Benefit | Risk and problem |
|---|---|
| saving time and costs | the **digital divide**: people without the Internet or skills |
| transparency, lower corruption risk | cybersecurity and personal data leaks |
| services available 24/7 | system outages |
| data-driven public administration | incompatibility between agencies' systems |
| a better business environment | a complicated user interface |

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Seeing e-government as just a website.** The main value is integration between agencies and redesigned processes.
- **Giving your digital signature key and password to others.** By law a digital signature equals a handwritten one.
- **Trusting fake "public service" websites.** Check the official domains (gov.uz).
- **Ignoring the digital divide.** Offline centres and a help service are needed too.
:::

:::tip
Register on my.gov.uz through OneID (or use demo mode) and open 3 services: which data is required, is it filled in automatically from inter-agency systems, how long does the result take?
:::

## Summary

- E-government is the interaction of the state with citizens, businesses and agencies through ICT (G2C, G2B, G2G, G2E).
- Stages: information → interaction → transaction → transformation (the once-only principle, proactive services).
- Infrastructure: a single portal, identification (OneID, digital signatures), integration, payments, open data.
- In Uzbekistan: my.gov.uz, the tax account, electronic invoices, public procurement, data.gov.uz.
- Benefits: time, transparency, 24/7; risks: the digital divide, security, outages.

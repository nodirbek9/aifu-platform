## Purpose of the seminar

In lecture M1 we became familiar with the course's basic concepts. In the seminar we apply them to **real economic situations**: we discuss how ICT creates value in different sectors, assess a company's digital maturity and draw up a digitalization plan for a small business.

:::note
**After this seminar you will be able to:**

- explain the role of ICT in economic sectors with examples;
- assess a company's digital maturity on a simple scale;
- estimate the effectiveness (ROI) of an ICT project;
- propose a digitalization plan for a small business.
:::

## ICT in economic sectors

| Sector | Use of ICT | Economic result |
|---|---|---|
| **Trade** | online cash registers, inventory accounting, online shops, marketplaces | fast service, precise stock management, new markets |
| **Banking and finance** | Internet banking, mobile apps, online lending, scoring | service without branches, lower costs |
| **Tax and government** | electronic reports, electronic invoices, my.gov.uz | transparency, time savings, lower corruption risk |
| **Manufacturing** | ERP, automated lines, sensors (IoT) | lower production cost, quality control |
| **Agriculture** | satellite and drone data, smart irrigation | higher yields, water savings |
| **Transport and logistics** | GPS tracking, route optimization, online ordering | fuel and time savings |

## Assessing digital maturity

A company's digital maturity can be assessed on a simple five-level scale:

| Level | Description |
|---|---|
| 1. Initial | records on paper or in separate files; little use of the Internet |
| 2. Basic | office programs, email, an online cash register, electronic tax reports |
| 3. Integrated | accounting, inventory and sales in one system; data in one place |
| 4. Digital channels | online sales, a mobile app, CRM, data-driven decisions |
| 5. Digital model | the business model is based on a digital platform, wide use of data analysis and automation |

## Return on ICT investment

**ROI** (Return on Investment) is the profitability ratio of an investment:

ROI = (benefit from the project − project cost) / project cost × 100%.

Example: a shop spent 20 million soums on an inventory program and scanners; during the year losses and excess stock fell, saving 30 million soums. ROI = (30 − 20) / 20 × 100% = 50%. A real calculation also considers several years of benefit, staff training costs and risks.

## Case: the "Bahor" family shop

"Bahor" is a grocery shop with 3 employees. Currently: sales are written in a cash book, stock is counted by hand once a month, orders to suppliers are made by phone, payments are mostly in cash. The owner complains that costs are rising and some products often run out.

**Group tasks**:

1. Determine the shop's digital maturity level and justify it.
2. Name the three most important problems and propose ICT tools to solve them (for example, an online cash register with an inventory program, QR payments, electronic ordering with suppliers).
3. Write down the approximate cost and expected benefit of each tool and calculate the ROI.
4. Draw up the implementation sequence and a staff training plan.
5. Name the risks (data loss, Internet outages, staff resistance) and the measures against them.

## Discussion questions

- Why can the same program be effective in one company and useless in another?
- Does digitalization cut jobs or create new ones? Give examples.
- What barriers does a small business face in digitalization (finance, knowledge, infrastructure)?
- What are the benefits and risks of cashless payments for the state and for entrepreneurs?

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Treating technology as the goal.** The business problem comes first, then the tool.
- **Seeing only the cost.** Time saved, reduced losses and new revenue are counted too.
- **Forgetting staff training.** If a program exists but is not used, the investment is lost.
- **Implementing everything at once.** Step-by-step implementation reduces risk.
:::

:::tip
For a company you know (a shop, a pharmacy, a training centre), determine its digital maturity level and propose the one ICT solution that would bring it the most benefit.
:::

## Summary

- ICT reduces costs, increases speed and quality and opens new markets in all economic sectors.
- Digital maturity is assessed from the initial level up to a digital business model.
- ICT investment is evaluated with ROI and TCO (M2).
- A digitalization plan: problem → tool → cost and benefit → step-by-step implementation → training → risks.

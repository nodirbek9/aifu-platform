## Purpose of this independent study

In M9 we got to know the concept of an information system, its types by management level (TPS, MIS, DSS, EIS) and its life cycle. In this independent study the question is different: **how does an information system create value for an organization**, and how does it turn into a competitive advantage?

:::note
**After this independent study you will be able to:**

- name the tasks of an information system at three levels of an organization;
- show the systems supporting the links of Porter's value chain;
- explain how information systems serve competitive strategies;
- know about IT governance and ways of measuring effectiveness.
:::

## Tasks at three levels

| Level | Task of the information system | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational** | performing daily operations quickly, accurately and cheaply | cash registers, warehouse accounting, payments |
| **Managerial** | information for control, planning and coordination | monthly reports, budget execution, KPIs |
| **Strategic** | new opportunities, competitive advantage, changing the business model | opening an online channel, a new data-driven product |

As a company develops, the role of its information systems usually grows from the bottom up: first automating bookkeeping, then management information, then a strategic tool.

## Porter's value chain

Michael Porter (1985) divided a company's activities into value-creating links. Information systems support each link:

| Link | Content | Information system |
|---|---|---|
| **Inbound logistics** | receiving and storing raw materials | a warehouse system (WMS), a supplier portal |
| **Operations** | turning them into products | MES, production planning (ERP) |
| **Outbound logistics** | delivery to the customer | order and delivery tracking (SCM) |
| **Marketing and sales** | creating demand and selling | CRM, an online shop, digital marketing (M13) |
| **Service** | after-sales service | a call centre, a chatbot, warranty records |
| *Support*: **infrastructure** | management, finance, legal | accounting, the ERP finance module |
| *Support*: **human resources** | hiring and developing staff | HRM, distance learning (LMS) |
| *Support*: **technology** | R&D, design | CAD, project management |
| *Support*: **procurement** | buying resources | electronic procurement, tenders |

The greatest value is often created in the links **between** activities: if sales data flows automatically into the production plan and purchasing, excess stock and shortages decrease.

## The five forces and information systems

| Competitive force | How information systems help |
|---|---|
| **New entrants** | a complex system and database raise the barrier to entry |
| **Substitute products** | analysing customer needs and launching new services quickly |
| **Buyer power** | loyalty programmes, personal offers — the "cost" of switching to another seller rises |
| **Supplier power** | electronic procurement and comparing many suppliers |
| **Rivalry among competitors** | cheaper processes, faster service, better analytics |

## Competitive strategies

| Strategy | Role of the information system | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost leadership** | automating processes, reducing stock | discount chains, automated warehouses |
| **Differentiation** | unique digital services | convenient features in a bank app |
| **Focus** | in-depth analysis of a specific segment | a special mobile loan for farmers |
| **Customer intimacy** | CRM, personalization, customer history | "Recommended for you" |

:::tip
A strategic advantage is lost quickly: competitors can buy the same software. A lasting advantage comes from a unique combination of **data, processes and people**, not from software alone.
:::

## Business process reengineering

Automating an old process ("making paper electronic") gives limited benefit. **Business process reengineering** (BPR) means radically rebuilding a process starting from what technology makes possible. An example: a loan application used to pass through 5 departments on paper and take 2 weeks to review; with a scoring system, electronic documents and parallel checks (the BPMN in MT11) a decision comes out in a few minutes.

## IT governance

- **CIO** (chief information officer) — links IT to business goals; **CDO** — chief data or digital officer.
- **The IT strategy** follows from the business strategy: first "what do we want to achieve?", then "which systems are needed?".
- **The IT portfolio** — selecting projects by value and risk.
- **COBIT** — an international framework for IT governance and control; **ITIL** — practices for IT service management.

## Measuring the impact

**The "productivity paradox"** (R. Solow, 1987): "You can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics." Later research showed that the impact comes when IT is accompanied by **organizational change** (processes, training, management) and with a delay of several years.

| Indicator | Example |
|---|---|
| **Financial** | ROI, TCO (M2), cost reduction |
| **Operational** | order fulfilment time, number of errors, operations per employee |
| **Customer** | satisfaction level, repeat purchases, app rating |
| **Strategic** | share of revenue from a new channel, market share |

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Thinking that buying a system = solving the problem.** Without changes in processes and people there is no impact.
- **Drawing up an IT strategy without a business strategy.** "Technology for technology's sake".
- **Measuring only costs.** Speed, quality and customer satisfaction are also value.
- **Automating an old chaotic process.** The chaos just speeds up.
:::

## Assignment

Choose a company you know (a bank, a retail chain, a telecom operator, a taxi service) and write a 2–3 page analysis:

1. Show which information systems are used for at least 5 links of the value chain.
2. Which competitive strategy does the company follow, and how do its information systems serve it?
3. Choose two of the five forces and explain how information systems protect the company.
4. Propose an idea for reengineering one process and write how its impact would be measured.

## Summary

- An information system works at three levels: operational efficiency, management information, strategic advantage.
- Porter's value chain is a map for placing systems by link; the greatest value lies between the links.
- Information systems are a tool for withstanding the five forces and implementing competitive strategies.
- The impact comes when processes and people change along with technology; it should be measured with financial, operational, customer and strategic indicators.

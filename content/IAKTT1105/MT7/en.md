## Purpose of this independent study

In M6 we saw the types of Internet services. This independent study is practical: the skills an economist needs to use the Internet **quickly, reliably and safely** in everyday work.

:::note
**After this independent study you will be able to:**

- find precise information quickly with search operators;
- assess the reliability of a source;
- write a business email correctly;
- share files in the cloud safely;
- organize video conferences and online surveys.
:::

## Effective search

| Technique | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Exact phrase — quotation marks | `"turnover tax"` | pages containing exactly this phrase |
| Excluding a word | `inflation -crypto` | results without the word "crypto" |
| By site | `site:stat.uz population` | only pages on stat.uz |
| By domain type | `site:gov.uz electronic services` | only government websites |
| File type | `filetype:pdf financial statements 2025` | only PDF documents |
| Or | `mortgage OR "housing loan"` | either of the two |

**Specialized sources**:

- **Official statistics**: stat.uz, the Central Bank (cbu.uz), the Ministry of Finance, the World Bank database (data.worldbank.org), the IMF.
- **Legislation**: lex.uz.
- **Scientific articles**: Google Scholar, open-access journals, the university library's electronic databases.
- **Company reports**: the "Investors" section of a company website, stock exchange websites.

:::tip
Do not replace search with artificial intelligence chatbots: they may give figures without sources or invented ones. Use a chatbot for ideas and direction, and take the figures from the primary source.
:::

## Evaluating sources: CRAAP

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| **Currency** | when was the information published and updated? |
| **Relevance** | does it answer my question? |
| **Authority** | who is the author? is the organization reliable? |
| **Accuracy** | are the sources of the figures given? do other sources confirm them? |
| **Purpose** | is the aim to inform, to sell or to persuade? |

**Fact-checking**: check a figure in at least two independent sources; go back to the original source (the statistics agency, not a news site); use reverse image search (Google Lens) for pictures; be wary of "urgent" and "sensational" headlines.

## Email and business correspondence

**The structure of a business email**:

```text
Subject:  Q3 2025 sales report — data request by 10 October

Dear Mr Rustamov,

The Finance Department is preparing the Q3 report. Please send the sales
data by branch in the attached form by 10 October.

If you have any questions, please call +998 XX XXX XX XX.

Kind regards,
Malika Karimova
Economist, Finance Department
```

**Rules**:

- A clear **subject**: what and by when.
- One email — one issue; the main request in the first paragraph.
- **"To"** — those who must act, **"Cc"** — those who should be informed; **"Bcc"** — so as not to reveal addresses in a mass mailing to many recipients.
- Use "Reply all" only when everyone really needs it.
- Send large files as a cloud link, not as an attachment.
- Send business emails from a corporate address, not a personal one.

## Cloud storage and sharing

| Sharing level | Who can see | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Restricted** (only named people) | invited accounts | internal and confidential documents |
| **Within the organization** | all of the organization's employees | general guidelines |
| **Anyone with the link** | anyone who gets the link | only public materials |

Permissions: **view**, **comment**, **edit**. The least privilege principle (M14): give everyone only the permission they need. Use expiring links and close access when a project ends. The version history helps undo a mistaken change.

## Video conferences

- **Tools**: Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Telegram.
- **For the organizer**: send the agenda and the link in advance, a waiting room and a password, managing participants' microphones, closing confidential windows before sharing the screen, recording decisions and tasks.
- **For a participant**: test the camera and microphone in advance, mute when not speaking, a quiet background.
- Ask participants for consent before recording.

## Online forms and surveys

With Google Forms, Microsoft Forms and similar tools, customer surveys, applications or registration forms are built quickly; responses go automatically into a table and are analysed in Excel (M4, MT2). A good survey: short (5–10 questions), one topic per question, no leading questions ("Our service is great, isn't it?" — wrong), personal data only when necessary and with consent.

## Browser hygiene and the digital footprint

- Keep the browser and extensions updated; install only the extensions you need.
- Use a password manager and multi-factor authentication (M14).
- On a shared computer, use "Incognito" mode and sign out after work.
- Check the address before signing in to a site (HTTPS and the correct domain, S14).
- **The digital footprint**: posts, comments and photos on social networks are kept for a long time — employers see them too. Separate a professional profile (for example, LinkedIn) from personal ones.

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Using the figure from the first page found.** Find the primary source.
- **A business email with no subject or with the subject "Hi".**
- **Sharing a confidential file in "anyone with the link" mode.**
- **Sending to all participants with their addresses visible in the "To" field.**
:::

## Assignments

1. Use the `site:` and `filetype:` operators to find the latest annual report (PDF) of the Central Bank of Uzbekistan.
2. Assess one news article about the inflation rate against the CRAAP criteria and check the figure in an official source.
3. Write a business email to a partner company proposing a meeting (subject, greeting, purpose, date, signature).
4. Create a folder in the cloud and share it with one classmate with "comment" permission only.
5. Create a 5-question customer satisfaction survey.

## Summary

- Search operators (quotation marks, `site:`, `filetype:`, `-`, `OR`) save time; figures are taken from primary official sources.
- Sources are assessed with the CRAAP criteria and checked in at least two sources.
- A business email: a clear subject, one issue, the right recipients, a signature.
- Cloud sharing means the least permission; video conferences and online forms have their own rules; mind your digital footprint.

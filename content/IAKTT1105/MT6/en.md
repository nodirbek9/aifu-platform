## Mechanisms of economic impact

| Mechanism | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Lower transaction costs** | searching, negotiating, documents and payments become cheaper | online public services — no queues or travel costs |
| **Less information asymmetry** | buyers and sellers see prices and quality | a farmer sees wholesale prices on the phone |
| **Market expansion** | a small firm sells across the country and abroad | craftsmen on marketplaces (M13) |
| **Higher productivity** | processes are automated, errors decrease | ERP (M15), online cash registers |
| **New business models** | platforms, subscriptions, remote services | taxi apps, online education |
| **Transparency** | corruption and the "shadow" economy shrink | electronic public procurement, electronic invoices |

## The network effect and Metcalfe's law

The **network effect** means a service's value grows as the number of users grows: one telephone is useless, two give one connection, and millions of telephones give enormous opportunities. **Metcalfe's law**: the number of possible connections in a network is n·(n − 1)/2, that is, it grows roughly with the square of the number of users.

| Users (n) | Possible pairwise connections n·(n − 1)/2 |
|---|---|
| 2 | 1 |
| 10 | 45 |
| 100 | 4,950 |
| 1,000 | 499,500 |

When users increase 10-fold (100 → 1,000), connections increase ~100-fold. That is why payment systems, messengers and marketplaces quickly become market leaders and a "winner takes most" situation arises — which also matters for competition policy.

:::tip
Metcalfe's law is an upper estimate: in reality not every connection is equally valuable. But the main conclusion holds — a network's value grows faster than its number of users.
:::

## Digital economy indicators

| Indicator | Who calculates it | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Share of Internet users** | ITU (International Telecommunication Union), national statistics | what percentage of the population uses the Internet |
| **Broadband connections and mobile coverage** | ITU | 4G/5G coverage, fibre connections per 100 people |
| **ICT share of GDP** | national statistics (stat.uz) | the contribution of the information and communication sector to the economy |
| **ICT Development Index (IDI)** | ITU | a composite index of ICT development |
| **E-Government Development Index (EGDI)** | the UN | the development of e-government (MT18) |
| **Share of digital payments** | the Central Bank, the World Bank (Global Findex) | cashless payments and the share of the population with an account |

When comparing indices, pay attention to the **methodology and the year**: an index's composition may change between years.

## The digital divide

The **digital divide** is the gap in the opportunity to use technology:

- **Geographic**: town and village, the centre and remote districts (coverage and speed).
- **Social**: income (the price of devices and tariffs), age (older people), gender.
- **Skills**: there is Internet access, but not the knowledge to use it effectively (digital literacy).

**Ways to narrow it**: building fibre and mobile networks in remote areas (public-private partnership), affordable tariffs and devices, digital literacy courses, making public services simple and mobile, help through service centres (MT19).

## "Digital Uzbekistan — 2030"

The strategy aims to develop digital infrastructure, e-government, the digital economy (IT services exports, IT Park), digital education and training of specialists. Main directions:

- covering settlements with broadband Internet and mobile communication;
- moving public services to electronic form and inter-agency integration;
- digitalizing sectors of the economy (industry, agriculture, transport, healthcare);
- developing the IT sector and increasing services exports;
- training programmers and digital specialists.

Follow the text of the strategy and its updates on Lex.uz and the website of the Ministry of Digital Technologies.

## Evaluating a connectivity project

For example, a project to connect 40 farms in a district to fibre Internet. Evaluation questions:

- **Costs**: construction, equipment, annual service (TCO, M2).
- **Benefits**: access to market prices (information asymmetry), online sales, smart irrigation and IoT (S6), applying for subsidies and loans online, saved travel and time.
- **Measurement**: comparing indicators before and after the project (income, selling price, losses) and, if possible, comparing with a neighbouring district that was not connected (a control group).

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Equating Internet coverage with digitalization.** Without skills and services the channel goes unused.
- **Comparing indices across different years and methodologies.**
- **Ignoring the network effect.** A small platform may fail to attract users even if it is better.
- **Counting only costs and forgetting the social benefit.** Saved time and access to services also have value.
:::

## Summary

- Networks reduce transaction costs, expand markets, and increase productivity and transparency.
- The network effect: value grows faster than the number of users (Metcalfe's law n·(n − 1)/2).

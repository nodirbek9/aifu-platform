## Purpose of the seminar

In lecture M10 we covered the structure and models of e-government. In the seminar we practise **using interactive services in practice** — registering, applying, paying and receiving the result — and assess service quality from the user's point of view.

:::note
**After this seminar you will be able to:**

- explain the concept and features of an interactive service;
- carry out the stages of using public and commercial interactive services;
- follow security rules when receiving a service;
- assess the quality of an interactive service by criteria.
:::

## What an interactive service is

An **interactive service** is a **two-way** interaction between a user and an organization through an electronic channel: the user submits an application, the system checks it, reports its status and provides the result electronically. Unlike a simple information website, the service can be received online from start to finish.

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Identification | the user's identity is confirmed (OneID, a digital signature, SMS, an ID card) |
| Online application | form data is filled in automatically, attachments are uploaded |
| Payment | the state fee or service charge online |
| Status tracking | "received", "under review", "ready" |
| Electronic result | a document confirmed with a digital signature or a certificate with a QR code |
| Feedback | rating, complaints, a help service |

## Types of interactive services

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Public services** | my.gov.uz (certificates, permits, registration), public service centres, electronic queues |
| **Tax services** | soliq.uz interactive services: checking tax debt, submitting reports, registering as self-employed |
| **Banking and payments** | mobile banking, Click, Payme, utility payments, online loans and deposits |
| **Education** | university portals, online admission, electronic gradebooks |
| **Healthcare** | booking a doctor online, electronic prescriptions |
| **Chatbots** | orders, certificates and service status via Telegram bots |

## Stages of receiving a service (a general scheme)

1. **Finding the official source** — the official domain (`gov.uz`, the bank's official app).
2. **Identification** — logging in through OneID, a digital signature key or an ID card.
3. **Choosing the service** and reading the requirements: needed data, deadline, price.
4. **Filling in the application** — checking the data, uploading attachments.
5. **Payment** (if required) — keeping the receipt.
6. **Tracking the status** — SMS, email or the personal account.
7. **Receiving the result** — downloading the electronic document, verifying it via the QR code.
8. **Rating** — rating the service quality or filing a complaint.

## Security rules

- Official websites and apps only; check the domain in the address bar (A9 of the KIA course).
- Never tell anyone an SMS code, your digital signature key password or a card's CVV — even if an "employee" calls.
- Log out after working on a public computer.
- Verify the result document through its QR code — protection against forged documents.

## Practical tasks

1. **A service map**: choose 3 services on my.gov.uz (for example a certificate of no criminal record, registering as an individual entrepreneur, a place in preschool education). For each one make a table: the needed data, payment, deadline, type of result.
2. **The tax service**: look through the list of interactive services on soliq.uz; choose the 3 most useful for an entrepreneur and justify your choice.
3. **A payment app**: find out which public services can be paid for in the payment app you use.
4. **Assessing quality**: rate the chosen service from 1 to 5 by the criteria below and make suggestions.

| Criterion | Question |
|---|---|
| Convenience | the number of steps, clarity, working on a mobile device |
| Speed | the time to fill in the application and receive the result |
| Transparency | status tracking, deadlines and price known in advance |
| Reliability | errors, outages |
| Help | a help service and instructions for questions |
| Security | identification, the possibility of verifying the result |

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Trusting the first link in search results.** It may be an advert or a fake site — check the official domain.
- **Submitting an application without checking it.** One wrong piece of data leads to rejection and delay.
- **Not keeping the payment receipt.** If there is a problem, the payment is hard to prove.
- **Sending personal data to an unknown "helper" via a messenger.** A risk of fraud.
:::

:::tip
You do not have to enter your personal data to study the services: many service pages show the requirements, deadline and price before logging in. Submit a real application only when you need to.
:::

## Summary

- An interactive service is a two-way interaction through an electronic channel: identification, application, payment, status, electronic result.
- Types: public, tax, banking and payment, education, healthcare services, chatbots.
- Stages of receiving a service: official source → identification → application → payment → tracking → result → rating.
- Security: official domains, keeping SMS codes and digital signature passwords secret, verifying the result via QR.

Q: How is the user's identity confirmed in public services?
A: Through OneID, a digital signature key, an ID card or SMS.

## Why does this topic matter?

Even the best analysis does not become a decision if you cannot present it clearly. An economist presents reports to management, business plans to a bank, projects to investors and research results at academic conferences. A good presentation is short, clearly structured and makes the numbers visual; a bad one consists of slides full of text and unreadable charts.

:::note
**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- know the main capabilities of presentation programs;
- use slide layouts, themes and the slide master;
- place tables and charts on slides correctly;
- build a clear presentation following design principles;
- know how to present and export a presentation.
:::

| **Apple Keynote** | macOS/iOS, attractive templates |

| **Canva** | ready-made designs, graphic elements, online |
| **Prezi** | non-linear, "zooming" presentations |

## Presentation structure

1. **Title slide**: topic, author, date, organization.
2. **Problem or goal**: why does it matter?
3. **Main part**: 3–5 key points, each with evidence (numbers, charts).
4. **Conclusion and proposal**: what should be done, a concrete proposal for a decision.
5. **A slide for questions** and contacts.

**For a business presentation (pitch)** a typical structure: problem → solution → market size → business model → competitors → financial forecast → team → the investment requested.

## Main capabilities

| Capability | How it is used |
|---|---|
| **Layout** | the placement of the title and content is ready; type text into the layout placeholders |
| **Theme** and **Slide Master** | the same font, colours and logo on all slides — changed in one place |
| **Tables and charts** | inserting them linked to Excel — when the data is updated, the chart is updated too |
| **SmartArt and shapes** | processes, hierarchies, diagrams |
| **Images, icons, video** | visual evidence; respect copyright |
| **Animations and transitions** | revealing information step by step; too many distract |
| **Speaker notes** | below the slide, visible only to you (Presenter View) |
| **Export** | PDF (an unchangeable handout), video, images |

## Design principles

- **One slide — one idea.** The title states the idea as a full sentence: "Sales grew by 18% in 2025", not "Sales dynamics".
- **Little text.** 3–5 short points per slide; details in the notes and the handout.
- **A readable font.** At least 24 pt for body text, contrasting colours (dark text on a light background).
- **Consistency.** One theme, 2–3 colours, 1–2 fonts.
- **Guy Kawasaki's 10/20/30 rule** (a recommendation for business pitches): 10 slides, 20 minutes, a font of at least 30 pt.
- **Simplifying charts**: only the needed data, highlighting the key number, showing the conclusion with arrows and labels.

**Presenting data visually**:

| Goal | Chart |
|---|---|
| change over time | line |
| comparing categories | bar or column |
| parts of a whole | pie or doughnut (few parts) or 100% stacked column |
| the relationship of two indicators | scatter |
| one key number | a large number and a short caption |

## Presenting and collaborating

- The slide show: `F5` (from the beginning), `Shift + F5` (from the current slide); in presenter mode the speaker sees the notes and the next slide.
- Rehearse the timing: about 1–2 minutes per slide.
- Backup: also save the file as a PDF, with copies on a flash drive and in the cloud.
- Collaboration: co-editing in the cloud (OneDrive, Google Drive), comments, version history.

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Filling a slide with text and reading it out.** The audience reads and does not listen to you.
- **Too many animations and transition effects.** They distract from the content.
- **An unreadable chart.** Small fonts, many colours and excess data.
- **Numbers without sources.** Give the source under every statistic.
- **Using an image from the Internet without permission.** Use sources with free licences.
:::

:::tip
Based on your latest report or essay, build a 5-slide presentation: each slide title should be a full sentence, and at least one chart should come from Excel.
:::

## Summary

- A presentation turns analysis into a decision: reports, business plans, pitches, project defenses.
- Programs: PowerPoint, Google Slides, Keynote, Impress, Canva, Prezi.
- Structure: title → problem → key points and evidence → conclusion and proposal → questions.
- Layouts, themes and the slide master ensure consistent design; tables and charts are linked to Excel.
- Design: one slide — one idea, little text, large fonts, contrast, the right chart type.

## Check yourself

:::check
Q: What is the slide master (Slide Master) for?
A: For setting and changing the font, colours and logo for all slides in one place.
Q: Which chart suits showing change over time?
A: A line chart.
Q: What should a good slide title be like?
A: It should state the key idea as a full sentence, for example "Sales grew by 18% in 2025".
Q: Who sees the speaker notes?
A: Only the speaker — in presenter mode.

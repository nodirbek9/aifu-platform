## Why does this topic matter?

A company performs thousands of operations every day: sales, purchases, payments, hiring. Recording these operations, preparing reports for managers from them and supporting decisions is the job of **information systems**. An economist is a user of an information system and often its customer: they determine which system is needed, what information it gives and how much it costs.

:::note
**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- explain the concepts of a system and an information system;
- name the functions and components of an information system;
- classify information systems by management level and function;
- know the purpose of ERP, CRM and SCM systems;
- name the life cycle of an information system and the risks of implementation.
:::

## Systems and information systems

A **system** is a set of interrelated elements serving a common goal (a company, a bank, a market). An **information system** (IS) is a set of people, processes, data and information technology that collects, stores, processes and distributes information for an organization's goals.

**Functions**:

| Function | Example (a shop) |
|---|---|
| **Input** | a product is scanned at the till |
| **Processing** | the amount, discount and tax are calculated |
| **Storage** | the sale is written to the database |
| **Output** | a receipt, a daily report, the stock balance |
| **Feedback** | when stock runs low — a recommendation to reorder |

**Components** (in detail in S9): hardware, software, data, networks, people, processes.

## Types by management level

| Level | System | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operational** | TPS — transaction processing system | recording daily operations | tills, banking operations, payroll |
| **Middle management** | MIS — management information system | regular reports | a monthly sales report, plan fulfilment |
| **Analysis and decisions** | DSS — decision support system | models, "what if" analysis | a lending decision, price scenarios |
| **Top management** | EIS — executive information system, dashboards | key indicators at a glance | a KPI panel, strategic indicators |

The lower level supplies the data and the upper level uses it in summarized form: every receipt at the till (TPS) → a monthly report (MIS) → a pricing policy model (DSS) → the manager's dashboard (EIS).

## Functional and enterprise systems

| System | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Accounting and finance | bookkeeping, reports, taxes, treasury |
| HR (HRM) | employees, payroll, leave, training |
| Marketing and sales | customers, orders, campaigns |
| Production | planning, materials, quality |
| **ERP** | combines all functions in a single database (M3) |
| **CRM** | manages customer relationships |
| **SCM** | the supply chain: suppliers, warehouses, logistics |

## The life cycle of an information system

1. **Planning**: the problem and the goal, a feasibility study (costs and benefits).
2. **Analysis**: user requirements, existing processes.
3. **Design**: the data structure, the interface, integration.
4. **Building or buying**: writing your own software or customizing a ready product.
5. **Testing and implementation**: data migration, training, go-live.
6. **Operation and support**: updates, improvements.
7. **Replacement or retirement**: when the system becomes obsolete.

**Effectiveness of implementation**: less time and fewer errors, faster and better decisions, cost savings; evaluated with ROI and TCO (S1, M2). **Risks**: exceeding the schedule and budget, user resistance, poor-quality data, dependence on the supplier, security.

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Seeing an information system as just a program.** Without people and processes the system does not work.
- **Choosing a system without defining requirements.** The result is a system that is unnecessary or insufficient.
- **Not training users.** Work continues the old way.
- **Ignoring data quality.** Wrongly entered data gives wrong reports.
:::

## Summary

- An information system is a set of people, processes, data and IT; its functions: input, processing, storage, output, feedback.
- Management levels: TPS (operations), MIS (reports), DSS (decisions), EIS (top management).
- Functional systems and the enterprise systems ERP, CRM, SCM.
- Life cycle: planning → analysis → design → building → implementation → operation → replacement.
- Effectiveness is evaluated with ROI and TCO; risks must be managed.

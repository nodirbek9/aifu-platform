Each country has chosen its own strategy according to its conditions — population, territory, initial infrastructure and political system.

## EGDI — the E-Government Development Index

Every two years the UN publishes the **E-Government Development Index** (EGDI). It consists of three equally weighted components:

| Component | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **Online Services Index (OSI)** | the completeness and quality of government portals and online services |
| **Telecommunication Infrastructure Index (TII)** | Internet users, mobile and broadband subscriptions |
| **Human Capital Index (HCI)** | literacy, the level and length of education |

In addition, the **E-Participation Index** assesses citizens' online participation. In recent rankings countries such as Denmark, Estonia, Singapore and South Korea lead. Uzbekistan has risen noticeably in the ranking over the last decade — check its current position in the latest UN report.

## Country experience

### Estonia — a "digital state"

- **X-Road** (2001) — a decentralized data exchange platform between public and private systems: the data stays in each agency but is exchanged through secure queries. This is the foundation of the "once-only" principle.
- **The digital ID card** and mobile ID — every citizen has an electronic signature, equal to a handwritten one.
- **e-Residency** (2014) — lets foreigners open and run an Estonian company online.
- Almost all public services are online; electronic voting (i-voting).
- A citizen can see which agency accessed their data and when — trust through transparency.

### Denmark — "digital by default" and mandatory digital post

- Citizens and companies correspond with the state mainly through **digital post** (Digital Post).
- **MitID** — a single digital identity for banking and public services.
- Exemptions and assistance remain for those without digital skills — the inclusiveness principle.

### South Korea — fast and comprehensive

- One of the world's most developed broadband infrastructures.
- The electronic public procurement system (KONEPS) — one of the first systems to make procurement fully electronic.
- A leader in the EGDI ranking for many years; exports its experience to other countries.

### Singapore — "Smart Nation"

- **Singpass** — a single digital identity used in hundreds of public and private services.
- **LifeSG** — services organized by "life events" (the birth of a child, benefits) in one app.
- Data-driven city management (transport, utilities).

### The United Kingdom — GOV.UK

- **GOV.UK** (2012) — a single government domain instead of hundreds of ministry and agency websites.
- **The Government Digital Service (GDS)** — a digital team inside government; "**digital by default**" and service standards (start with user needs, plain language, testing).
- The lesson: test services with users and keep improving them.

### India — "India Stack"

- **Aadhaar** — a biometric digital identifier issued to more than 1.3 billion people.
- **UPI** — an instant payment system; one of the largest real-time payment systems in the world.
- **DigiLocker** — a store of digital documents (diplomas, certificates).
- The "digital public infrastructure" (DPI) approach: the state builds open platforms and private companies build services on top of them.

### Kazakhstan — eGov.kz

- The **eGov.kz** portal and mobile app; most public services are online.
- **Public service centres** (TsON) — an offline "one-stop shop", similar to the public service centres in Uzbekistan.
- Public services through banking apps — cooperation with the private sector.

### The UAE — paperless government

- Dubai's "paperless strategy" — eliminating paper in the government's internal processes.
- Experiments with artificial intelligence and blockchain in public services (M12, S12).

## Success factors

| Factor | Example |
|---|---|
| **A single digital identity** | the Estonian ID, Singpass, MitID, Aadhaar |
| **An interoperability platform** | X-Road |
| **Strong political leadership and central coordination** | GDS, digital ministries |
| **User-centred design** | the GOV.UK standards, LifeSG |
| **Trust and transparency** | viewing the access log in Estonia |
| **Inclusiveness** | exemptions in Denmark, TsON in Kazakhstan |
| **Cooperation with the private sector** | India Stack, services through banking apps |

## Causes of failure

- Each agency builds its own system separately — no integration, and the citizen still carries paper.
- The technology exists, but processes and legislation have not changed (paper is still required).
- A complex interface and low digital literacy — services are little used.
- Insufficient attention to cybersecurity — a data leak destroys trust.
- Dependence on a single vendor — changes are expensive and slow.

## Lessons for Uzbekistan

1. **Base registers and integration** — fully implementing the "once-only" principle (MT17).
2. **A single digital identity** (OneID, a mobile digital signature) — simple and secure access to all services.
3. **Integrated services by life event** — several services with one application.
4. **Mobile first** — most of the population goes online by phone.
5. **Inclusiveness** — public service centres and assistance remain (the digital divide in MT6).
6. **Service quality indicators** — take-up, completion time and user ratings are published openly.

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Copying another country's solution directly.** Solutions differ for Estonia with 1.3 million people and India with 1.4 billion; take population, territory and infrastructure into account.
- **Turning a ranking position into the goal.** What matters is the real result for citizens.
- **Seeing only the technology.** Success comes together with legislation, organizational change and trust.
:::

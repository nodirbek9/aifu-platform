## Purpose of this independent study

The course consists of 15 lectures, 15 seminars and 31 independent study topics. Studying them in isolation is not very effective — the topics build on one another. In this independent study you draw up the course map, set a semester plan and choose your learning methods.

**After this independent study you will be able to:**

- show the course sections and the links between them;
- draw up a personal semester plan;
- apply effective learning methods;
- use the lessons, quizzes and tasks on the platform correctly.
:::

## Effective learning methods

| Method | How to do it | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Active recall** | after reading a lesson, close it and write down the main ideas; answer the "Check yourself" questions without looking at the answers | trying to remember strengthens memory |
| **Spaced repetition** | briefly review a topic after 1 day, 1 week and 1 month | slows down the forgetting curve |
| **Explaining in your own words** | explain the topic in simple language to a friend or to yourself | gaps in understanding show up immediately |
| **Doing the examples yourself** | rewrite the Excel and program examples without copying, change the input data | skills form only through practice |
| **Connecting** | link a new topic to earlier ones and to real life (your bank app, the tax account) | knowledge becomes a system |

**Not recommended**: only rereading and highlighting — this gives a false feeling of "I know it".

## Using the platform

- **Lesson text** — every lecture, seminar and independent study topic is available in Uzbek, Russian and English. Look at the terms in another language too — much of the professional literature is in English.
- **Code examples** — in Python, Java and C++; you can run them on the platform and check the result.
- **Quizzes** (in seminars) — in questions with several correct answers, select all the correct options.
- **Programming tasks** (S8, S15) — follow the input and output format exactly; first calculate a small example by hand.
- **"Common mistakes"** blocks — the mistakes made most often in exams.

## Notes and a glossary

Keep one page of notes per topic: 3–5 main ideas, 1 table or diagram, 1 example. Build a separate **glossary**: the term — in three languages — a short definition. For example:

| Term (uz) | ru | en | Definition |

| Elektron raqamli imzo | электронная цифровая подпись | electronic digital signature | an attribute confirming a document's author and integrity |

## Self-assessment

After each topic give yourself a mark from 1 to 5: 1 — I did not understand, 3 — I understood but cannot do it on my own, 5 — I can explain it to others. Discuss topics rated 3 or lower with the teacher or ask about them at the seminar.

## Academic integrity

Quizzes and programming tasks measure your knowledge. Copying someone else's solution or having artificial intelligence write it entirely means cheating yourself: you will need this skill in exams and at work. Use artificial intelligence to ask for explanations, and write the solution yourself.

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Leaving everything to the week before the exam session.** 61 topics cannot be mastered in a week.
- **Reading only the lectures and skipping seminars and independent study.** That is where the practical skills are.
- **Reading code examples without running them.** Programming is learned only hands-on.
- **Not asking about what you did not understand.** Small gaps grow in later topics.
:::

## Why does this course matter?

Today a large part of an economist's work is done on a computer: reporting and analysis spreadsheets, accounting programs, banking and tax systems, electronic document exchange, online trade. A company's competitiveness often depends on how quickly it collects and processes information and uses it in decision-making. This course teaches an economist to use modern information technologies and systems **consciously and effectively**.

:::note
**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- know the aims, tasks and structure of the course;
- distinguish the concepts of data, information and knowledge;
- name the properties of information as an economic resource;
- explain the components of ICT and of an information system;
- know the directions of the digital economy and of digital transformation in Uzbekistan.
:::

## Data, information and knowledge

| Concept | Definition | Economic example |
|---|---|---|
| **Data** | unprocessed facts, numbers, symbols | every receipt at the till: date, product, price |
| **Information** | data organized and given meaning for a purpose | a monthly sales report: how much of each product was sold |
| **Knowledge** | conclusions drawn from analyzing information and from experience | "drinks sales rise by 30% at weekends — stock must be increased" |

**Quality properties of information**: reliability (correct), timeliness (on time), completeness, accuracy, clarity, accessibility. A decision based on outdated or wrong information causes losses.

**Information as an economic resource**: it does not diminish when used; copying is almost free; its value depends on time and context; it must be protected (a leak of confidential data is a loss). That is why data is sometimes called "the new oil" in the modern economy.

## ICT and information systems

**Information and communication technologies (ICT)** are the set of methods, hardware and software used to collect, store, process, transmit and present information.

An **information system** is a system that combines ICT, data, people and processes to serve an organization's goals (in detail in M9).

| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Hardware** | computers, servers, cash registers, scanners, phones (M2) |
| **Software** | operating systems, office programs, accounting and ERP systems (M3) |
| **Networks and communication** | local networks, the Internet, mobile communication (M6) |
| **Data** | databases of customers, products, transactions (M7) |
| **People** | users, IT specialists, managers |
| **Processes** | procedures for accepting an order, preparing a report, approving a payment |

## The digital economy

The **digital economy** is economic activity based on digital technologies and data: e-commerce, online payments, platforms (taxis, delivery), digital public services, remote work.

**Main indicators**: the share of Internet users, the volume of cashless payments, e-commerce turnover, the number of digital public services, the share of the ICT sector in GDP, exports of IT services. International rankings: the UN E-Government Development Index (EGDI), ITU ICT development indicators.

**Digital transformation in Uzbekistan**: the "Digital Uzbekistan — 2030" strategy (2020) provides for digitalizing public administration, economic sectors and the social sphere, developing telecommunications infrastructure and training IT personnel. Practical results: the Single Portal of Interactive Public Services (my.gov.uz), the electronic account of the tax service, online cash registers and electronic invoices, payment and trade platforms such as Click, Payme and Uzum, IT Park and IT services exports.

## An economist's ICT competences

- office programs: calculation and analysis in spreadsheets (M4), presentations (M5);
- working with data: retrieving, sorting and summarizing data from databases (M7);
- algorithmic thinking: breaking a problem into steps and automating it (M8);
- using information systems and e-government services (M9, M10);
- assessing modern technologies: cloud, blockchain, e-business (M11–M13);
- following information security rules (M14).

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Treating data and information as the same thing.** Raw numbers must be processed to become useful for a decision.
- **Understanding ICT as computers only.** Without people, data and processes, technology gives no results.
- **Working with outdated information.** Timeliness matters in economic decisions — always check the date of the data.
- **Seeing digitalization only as "buying software".** Processes and staff skills must change too.
:::

:::

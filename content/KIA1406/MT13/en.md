## Why does this topic matter?

Almost every organization is going digital — banks, public services, telecoms, industry — and all of them need specialists who protect information. International studies (for example, ISC2's annual reports) note a worldwide shortage of millions of cybersecurity specialists. But "cybersecurity" is not a single profession; it is dozens of different directions: one person analyzes logs, another reviews code, another works with policies and audits. Choosing your path early makes your studies purposeful.

:::note
**After this assignment you will be able to:**

- name the main directions and positions in cybersecurity;
- know the knowledge and skills needed for each direction;
- plan your preparation for an entry-level position;
- build a portfolio and practical experience in legal ways;
- draw up a personal development plan.

## Main directions

| Direction | What it involves | Typical positions |
|---|---|---|
| **Monitoring and defense** (Blue team, SOC) | watching logs and alerts, detecting incidents | SOC analyst (L1–L3), threat hunter |
| **Incident response and forensics** | containing an incident, finding its causes, collecting evidence | incident responder, digital forensic analyst |
| **Security engineering and architecture** | designing and configuring protection: firewalls, IAM, cloud | security engineer, cloud security engineer, architect |
| **Application security** (AppSec, DevSecOps) | secure programming, code review, fixing vulnerabilities | AppSec engineer, secure developer |
| **Authorized testing** (penetration testing, Red team) | testing defenses under a contract and with permission, reporting | penetration tester, security auditor |
| **Governance, risk and compliance** (GRC) | policies, risk assessment, standards (ISO 27001), audit | GRC analyst, CISO |
| **Cryptography and research** | designing and analyzing algorithms and protocols | cryptographer, researcher |
| **Education and awareness** | training staff, workshops | security trainer |

The **NICE framework** (the Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity of the US NIST) describes the field in terms of tasks, knowledge and skills — you can use it to find suitable positions and the knowledge they require.

## Entry-level positions and a roadmap

Most specialists enter the field through another branch of IT: as a system administrator, network engineer, developer or support technician. This is a good path: to protect a system you first need to know how it works.

| Stage | What is learned | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1. IT basics | operating systems (Windows, Linux), networks (TCP/IP, DNS), programming (Python) | being able to configure systems independently |
| 2. Security basics | this course: risks, cryptography, authentication, access control, network protection | the core concepts and tools |
| 3. Choosing a direction | in-depth courses in SOC, cloud, AppSec or GRC | a first specialization |
| 4. Practice | labs, competitions, an internship, your own projects | a portfolio |
| 5. First job | a junior position, working with a mentor | real experience |

## Required skills

- **Technical**: administering Linux and Windows, networks, scripting (Python, PowerShell, Bash), cloud basics, log analysis, basic cryptography.
- **Analytical**: breaking a problem into parts, forming a hypothesis and testing it with evidence.
- **Communication**: explaining a risk clearly to a non-technical manager, writing a precise report (MT2).
- **English**: most standards, documentation and the community are in English.
- **Ethics**: keeping confidentiality, acting only with permission, obeying the law.
- **Continuous learning**: technologies and threats change every year.

## Portfolio and legal practice

For an employer "what I did" matters more than "what I know". For a portfolio:

- **A home lab**: a small network, a domain and centralized log collection in virtual machines; keep notes of what you did.
- **Learning platforms**: TryHackMe, Hack The Box Academy, Cisco Networking Academy, Microsoft Learn — labs built on purpose, where practice is permitted.
- **CTF competitions** (Capture The Flag): team challenges — cryptography, forensics, network analysis.
- **Open source projects** and GitHub: security scripts and documentation you wrote.
- **Blogs and reports**: writing explanations of what you learned — this also shows communication skills.

Remember: "practising" on someone else's system is a crime (M3). Use only authorized platforms.

## Opportunities in Uzbekistan

Banks and payment systems, telecom companies, government bodies and their information security services, IT companies and IT Park residents, as well as remote work for international companies are the main employers. Because the Law "On Cybersecurity" (2022) set security requirements for critical information infrastructure, demand for such specialists keeps growing. University internships, IT Park programmes and CTF competitions are good ways to gain experience.

## Personal development plan

Draw up a one-page plan for yourself:

1. Which 1–2 directions interest you, and why?
2. Find 3 real job openings in this direction (local and international) — which requirements repeat?
3. A list of the skills you already have and those you lack.
4. Concrete steps for the next 6 months: courses, labs, one portfolio project.
5. How will you measure the result (for example, the number of completed labs, written reports)?

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Only dreaming of being a "hacker".** Penetration testing is one of many directions; most jobs are in defense, engineering and GRC.
- **Skipping the IT basics.** Without knowing networks and operating systems you cannot understand security in depth.
- **Relying only on certifications.** A certificate without practical experience shows quickly in an interview.
- **Unauthorized "practice".** Criminal liability and loss of reputation in the field.
- **Neglecting communication skills.** If you cannot explain a problem you found, it will not be fixed.
:::

:::tip
On LinkedIn or a similar network, look at the profiles of 5 specialists in the position that interests you: which position did they start from, which skills do they show? This is the most realistic roadmap.
:::

## Summary

- Cybersecurity is a multi-direction field: SOC, incident response, engineering, AppSec, penetration testing, GRC, research.
- The path: IT basics → security basics → a direction → practice → first job.
- Besides technical skills, analytical thinking, communication, English and ethics matter.
- A portfolio: a home lab, learning platforms, CTFs, open projects; only legal platforms.
- Demand is growing in Uzbekistan: banks, telecoms, government bodies, IT companies.

## Why does this topic matter?

Independent work is a large part of the course: for the MT3–MT17 topics you prepare an essay, a presentation or a practical report. The daily work of a cybersecurity specialist consists of exactly such documents: an incident report, a risk assessment, a draft policy, an auditor's conclusion. A good document is clearly structured, based on evidence and understandable to its reader. In this assignment we look at how to plan, write and submit independent work.

:::note
**After this assignment you will:**

- know the types of independent work and the requirements for each;
- be able to write a report following a clear structure;
- be able to choose reliable sources and cite them correctly;
- know the rules for using artificial intelligence tools honestly;
- be able to check your own work against the assessment criteria.
:::

## Types of independent work

| Type | Size | What is required |
|---|---|---|
| **Essay** | 6–10 pages | a systematic account of the topic, at least 5 sources, a conclusion |
| **Presentation** | 10–15 slides, 7–10 minutes | the main ideas, diagrams and tables, answering questions |
| **Case analysis** | 3–5 pages | a description of the incident, causes, the violated property, measures (the model from A3, A12) |
| **Lab report** | 3–6 pages | aim, environment, steps performed (with screenshots), result, conclusion |
| **Programming task** | on the platform | code in one of three languages that works and passes the tests |

## Report structure

1. **Title page**: university, course, topic, student's name and group, date.
2. **Introduction**: the relevance of the topic (2–3 sentences) and the aim of the work.
3. **Main part**: 2–4 sections, each with a heading. Evidence for every claim: a source, an experiment result or an example.
4. **Practical part** (if any): the environment (OS, tool versions), the steps, screenshots of the results and their explanation.
5. **Conclusion**: the main findings and recommendations — no new information is added.
6. **References**: a list of all sources cited in the text.

**Writing rules**: short sentences; a term is explained when first used; tables and figures are numbered and referred to in the text; passwords, personal data and IP addresses are hidden in screenshots.

## Reliable sources

| Source | Why |
|---|---|
| **NIST** (csrc.nist.gov) | standards and guides: cryptography, passwords, risks, incident response |
| **CISA** (cisa.gov) | current threats, alerts, practical recommendations |
| **OWASP** (owasp.org) | web application security, the Top 10 |
| **ENISA** (enisa.europa.eu) | European threat landscape reports |
| **lex.uz** | the laws and regulations of Uzbekistan |
| **Vendor documentation** | Microsoft Learn, Linux man pages, official tool websites |
| **Research papers** | Google Scholar, IEEE, ACM |

**Evaluating a source**: who the author is and their expertise; the date (in cybersecurity information older than 5 years is often outdated); whether there is evidence and references; whether there is an advertising or sales purpose. Social media posts and anonymous forums cannot be a main source.

**Citing**: a number in square brackets in the text [1], a list at the end: author or organization, title, year, URL and access date. For example: [1] NIST. SP 800-63B: Digital Identity Guidelines. 2025. https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/ (accessed: 2026-10-05).

## Plagiarism and artificial intelligence

- Every idea, table or figure taken from another source is cited; word-for-word copying only in quotation marks and with a citation.
- Artificial intelligence tools (chatbots) may be used to **find ideas, explain and edit text**, but: verify the result against sources (they can confidently "invent" wrong facts and non-existent references); state in the work which tool was used and for what; the final text and conclusions must be your own.
- In a programming task, if you cannot explain copied or fully generated code, the task is not considered done.
- Do not enter confidential data (passwords, personal data, workplace documents) into online tools.

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Weight | For a high mark |
|---|---|---|
| Content and correctness | 40% | the topic is fully covered, no factual errors, correct terms |
| Analysis and conclusions | 25% | not just description: comparison, cause and effect, well-founded recommendations |
| Sources | 15% | at least 5 reliable, current sources, correct citations |
| Structure and formatting | 10% | a clear structure, numbered tables and figures, literate language |
| Defense | 10% | clear answers to questions in your own words |

:::warning
**Common mistakes**

- **Rewriting a single source.** That is not analysis; compare at least several sources.
- **Statistics without a citation.** Numbers such as "attacks grew by 300%" only with a source.
- **Confidential data in screenshots.** Hide passwords, tokens, personal data and IP addresses.
- **Copying an AI answer without checking.** Wrong facts and non-existent references show up immediately during assessment.
- **Leaving it to the last day.** The practical part (virtual machine, tools) needs time for unexpected problems.
:::

:::tip
Read your report aloud before submitting it: unclear or long sentences stand out immediately. To prepare for the defense, ask yourself 5 questions about your work and write 1–2 sentence answers.
:::

## Summary

- Types of independent work: essay, presentation, case analysis, lab report, programming task.
- Report: title page, introduction, main part, practical part, conclusion, references.
- Sources: NIST, CISA, OWASP, ENISA, lex.uz, official documentation; a citation for every idea.
- Artificial intelligence is an assistant, not the author: verify the result and state that you used it.
- Assessment: content, analysis, sources, formatting and defense.

## Check yourself

:::check
Q: What should the conclusion of a report contain?
A: The main findings and recommendations; no new information is added.
Q: How do you judge the reliability of a source?
A: By the author and their expertise, the publication date, the presence of evidence and references, and the absence of a commercial purpose.
Q: What should you observe when using an artificial intelligence tool?
A: Verify the result against sources, state that it was used, do not enter confidential data, and write the final text yourself.
Q: What must be hidden in the screenshots of a lab report?
A: Passwords, tokens, personal data and IP addresses.
:::
